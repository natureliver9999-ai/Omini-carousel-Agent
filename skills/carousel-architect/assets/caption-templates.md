# Caption templates

Captions do two jobs: they carry the keyword CTA, and they give the algorithm its text signal. Keep line breaks — mobile readers scan.

## Instagram — save-driven

```
{One-sentence restatement of the deck's claim.}

{One sentence on what the deck actually gives them: the framework, the checklist, the number.}

{Three or four short lines, one per slide beat — this is what the reader skims before swiping.}

Save this for {the moment they will need it}.
```

## Instagram — comment keyword

```
{Claim.}

{What is inside, in one sentence.}

Comment {KEYWORD} and I will send you {the artifact: the template / the spec / the checklist}.
No gate, no email — just the file.
```

## Instagram — hot take

```
{The opinion, stated flatly.}

{The one piece of evidence that makes it defensible.}

{What you are not saying, so the comment section argues with the right thing.}
```

## LinkedIn — document post

```
{The finding, in one line. Not the topic.}

{Two or three lines of context that make the finding surprising — this is the whole hook on LinkedIn.}

What the deck covers:
1. {Finding 1} — {source, sample size, date}
2. {Finding 2} — {source, sample size, date}
3. {Finding 3} — {source, sample size, date}

{What you want the reader to do differently this week.}

{Question a specific person can answer, not "thoughts?"}
```


## Rules

1. Never put a URL in the slide art for LinkedIn; never put one in an Instagram caption and expect a click.
2. The keyword must be one word, uppercase, and appear exactly twice (deck + caption).
3. Hashtags go last, on their own line.
4. No emoji in the art; emoji in the caption is fine and human.
5. Keep a blank line between blocks — the caption is scanned, not read.
